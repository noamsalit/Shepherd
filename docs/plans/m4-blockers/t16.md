## T16-1 · Four of the seven members cannot reach their data without importing something the task's own check forbids · owner: T16, resolved by injection, recorded as a deviation from the `Produces` block

**Symptom.** Task 16's `Produces` block lists seven members. Two of them — `master_tools()` and
`call()` — sit straight on `export.py`: same layer, downward import, done. **The other four have no
legal source of data inside L4:**

| member | where the fact lives | why the client may not import it |
|---|---|---|
| `autonomy_level()` | `store.get_app_state("autonomy_level")` | `Store` is one of the three names `test_the_client_exposes_no_store_and_no_registry` **is specified to go red on** |
| `wake_text()` | `orchestration.wake.render_wake_text(drain(...))` | `orchestration/` is **L3**; L4 importing it is an upward import (§5.0) |
| `withdraw_approval()` | `approvals.py` | it did not exist in the tree when this task ran (§T16-4) |
| `withdraw_turn_approvals()` | `approvals.py` | same |

The task's own Required Check and the task's own `Produces` block cannot both be satisfied by a
module of seven plain functions. Something has to arrive from outside.

**What shipped: three added members, and the argument for why they are not a widening of D19.**

```
bind_master_client(*, withdraw_approval, withdraw_turn_approvals, autonomy_level, wake_text)
reset_master_client()
MasterClientUnbound
```

**The `Produces` block is the master's budget, and these three are not on the master's side of the
seam.** `master/` calls the four verbs; the **composition root** calls `bind_master_client`. D19's
sentence is *"the master reaches the rest of the system only through the tool-surface client"* — it
bounds what `master/` may reach, and a root-side installer grants `master/` nothing. The four
callables arrive as `Callable[[str], bool]`, `Callable[[str], int]`, `Callable[[], int]` and
`Callable[[], str | None]`: **opaque, and no type below L4 is named anywhere in the file.** The
client's import set, asserted as an equality, is five modules and eight symbols, and `Store`,
`ToolDef` and `registered_tools` are in none of them.

It is also the **shape this tree already uses three times**: `orchestration/master_turn.py` takes
`publish` and `withdraw_turn_approvals` as injected callables for exactly this reason (its own
docstring: *"L3 may not import upward, so the two L4 callables arrive as parameters"*);
`registry.py` and `stream.py` are the other two L4 module-globals and both ship a public
`reset_*`.

**The alternative that was rejected, out loud.** `class MasterClient`, constructed by the root with
its four callables and handed to `master/` as an instance, is a **smaller** public surface — two
names (`MasterClient`, `MASTER_AUDIENCE`) against ten — and it needs no module global, no unbound
state, no `reset_*` and no typed refusal. It was rejected because the plan writes the seven members
as module-level `def`s with pinned signatures and T20 and T21 are unbuilt: a builder reading the
`Produces` block would find it false of the tree. **If the router prefers the class, the trade is
eight public names for one structural deviation, and T20 has not yet been written against either.**

**`MasterClientUnbound` is typed, not a bare `RuntimeError`** (RD-T17-2). And unbound is a *refusal*,
not a default: a client that answered `autonomy_level() == 2` because nobody wired it would ask for
every destructive call on a level-3 build — a misconfiguration rendered as a policy, and silent.

## T16-3 · `call()` is the only member with a hidden argument, and the audience is it · owner: T16, this is the module's whole deletion-test answer

The plan's *"why a module and not import `export` and `registry` directly"* is an allow-list
argument. There is a second, sharper one, and it is what the mutation table proves: **inline
`client.call()` at its call sites and the complexity does not vanish — it reappears in `master/` as
a `CallerContext` the master constructs for itself.** A caller that can choose its own audience has
no audience, and `Audience.HUMAN` is an unauthenticated self-stamp by `policy.py`'s own docstring.
`MASTER_AUDIENCE` is pinned once, and M1 (below) shows a one-word change to it opens the `HUMAN`
surface to the orchestrator agent and goes red in two checks.

Same reasoning for the absence of any per-tool convenience: the master calls tools **by name through
one function**, which is what makes the audience filter total rather than a property of however many
wrappers happen to exist.

## T16-7 · The client ships with **no caller** — a fourth row for the "no caller" register · owner: decide before M4 acceptance

`bind_master_client()` is called from nowhere. `compose_tool_surface()` is not on T16's file list
(and editing it would be a change to a module T16 does not own), and `master/sdk_tools.py` (T20) and
`master/runtime.py` (T21) are unbuilt — so all seven members are reachable only from
`tests/toolsurface/test_client.py`. That is the exact pattern router-decisions names: *a mechanism
with passing tests and no caller counts as evidence at acceptance while doing nothing*, three rows
already, and M3's uncalled RFC 6455 parser before them.

**The two lines that close it**, named as the register requires:

```python
# in toolsurface/compose.py, after freeze_registry():
bind_master_client(
    withdraw_approval=approvals.withdraw,               # T4
    withdraw_turn_approvals=approvals.withdraw_turn,    # T4
    autonomy_level=lambda: int(store.get_app_state("autonomy_level") or 2),
    wake_text=lambda: render_wake_text(drain(store, utc_now)) or None,
)
```

**Two things the owner of that edit must not do without deciding first.** (a) `wake_text` above
**drains**, and `master_turn.TurnDriver._opening_text` already drains once per turn — two drains is
D31's lost stop, which is the defect T27's single stamp exists to prevent. Either the client's
`wake_text` is the driver's (and the driver stops draining) or one of them is cut. **T16's reading
is that `wake_text()` is currently redundant with T27 and is the member most likely to be the one
that should go.** (b) `compose.py` lives at L4 and importing `orchestration.wake` there is the same
upward import §T16-1 is about — the lambda has to be built by whoever owns the root above L4, not
inside `compose_tool_surface`.
